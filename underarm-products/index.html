The Ordinary and PanOxyl for underarm odor?

Reddit users discuss these products for underarm odor. One is an exfoliating toner; the other is an acne cleanser. Here is how their labeled uses differ from those routines.

Read the intended use before trying a social-media routine. Neither product is presented here as a genital cleanser or a proven menopause-odor treatment. This page does not provide instructions for using them outside their labels.

The Ordinary Glycolic Acid 7% Exfoliating Toner

What it is: A leave-on exfoliating toner, not a rinse-off body wash. The product label covers the face, neck and scalp; the brand also discusses small rough or bumpy areas of body skin.

What this means for odor: Underarm odor is the use discussed on Reddit. The manufacturer material reviewed does not establish that this toner treats menopause-related odor.

Before considering it: Avoid sensitive, peeling or damaged skin. It can irritate skin and increase sun sensitivity.

Read the label precautions

The label calls for patch testing, avoiding the eyes, and using only on unbroken skin. If irritation occurs, rinse, stop use and consult a physician. Follow the label’s sun-protection precautions during use and for one week afterwards.

PanOxyl Acne Treatment Bar · 10% Benzoyl Peroxide

What it is: A rinse-off acne-treatment bar with 10% benzoyl peroxide. Its labeled uses cover acne on the face, chest and back, with shoulders also mentioned in the brand FAQ.

What this means for odor: This is an acne medication. The label reviewed does not give instructions for underarm-odor treatment. The 10% bar is different from the 4% creamy wash and the 10% foaming wash discussed elsewhere.

Before considering it: Benzoyl peroxide can irritate skin and bleach hair or fabric. Check that you are looking at the bar and the intended strength.

Read the label precautions

For external use only. Avoid very sensitive skin and do not use if sensitive to benzoyl peroxide. Avoid the eyes, lips and mouth. Stop and seek medical advice for severe irritation. The label advises sun protection and asks pregnant or breastfeeding users to consult a health professional. Check the current carton for full directions and ingredients.
