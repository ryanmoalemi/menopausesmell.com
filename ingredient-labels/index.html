What to check on a wash label

Check where it is meant to be used, whether it contains fragrance, and what its claims actually tell you.

Sources checked November 12, 2026 · Not clinically reviewed

Start with intended use

Check whether a product is a body cleanser, an external vulva cleanser, an oral supplement, or a vaginal product. These routes are not interchangeable. Our wash comparison covers external products only.

“Sensitive” does not mean fragrance-free

We checked the exact current variants, not just the brand. Dove’s Ultra Sensitive Body Wash says fragrance-free and lists 10 ingredients. Its ordinary Sensitive Body Wash lists fragrance. Similar names can hide a meaningful formula difference.

“Natural” can still include a scent

Good Clean Love Balance lists Natural Lavender Flavor and describes a floral aroma. That is different from fragrance-free. Botanical ingredients can still matter to someone avoiding scented products.

When a page contradicts itself

At our November 12, 2026 check, the Vagisil Menopause Support Probiotic Mousse Wash page included fragrance-free wording, “light botanical scent,” and Fragrance in the ingredient list. We treat the fragrance status as conflicting and do not describe it as fragrance-free while those details conflict.

What this means for shopping

Use the current package and the complete ingredient list. If the label and page disagree, ask the manufacturer before buying. We do not resolve a contradiction by choosing the most favorable claim.

Product pH is not a diagnosis

A bottle’s “pH balanced” wording does not show that it changes internal vaginal pH or treats symptoms. Vaginal pH may rise after menopause. A home pH reading cannot identify the cause of an odor or reliably rule out infection.

“Probiotic” on a wash is a separate claim

A listed ferment does not, by itself, establish living probiotic organisms, viable count, or clinical benefit. Evidence for an oral or vaginal formulation cannot simply be applied to a rinse-off cleanser.
