When vaginal odor needs medical attention

A change in odor with discharge, itching, burning or pain is a reason to seek advice. This page explains when to arrange an appointment and when to get urgent help.

Sources checked November 12, 2026 · Not clinically reviewed

Arrange a clinician’s assessment

Bring up new or persistent vaginal odor, unusual discharge, itching, burning, pain when urinating, discomfort with sex, or symptoms that keep returning. You do not need to be certain of the cause before making an appointment.

Bleeding after menopause needs evaluation

Any vaginal bleeding after menopause should be assessed, even if it happens once or is only a small amount.

When to seek urgent help

Pelvic pain with fever or unusual discharge needs urgent assessment. Severe or worsening pelvic pain, fainting, or heavy bleeding warrants emergency help. This list is not exhaustive; seek help if you feel seriously unwell.

What you can bring

Write down when the change began, other symptoms, whether it comes and goes, and the products or treatments you use. A picture of a product’s ingredient label can be more useful than remembering the brand name alone.

While you are waiting

Keep external care gentle. Avoid trying to cover the smell with fragrance or using a pH strip as a diagnosis. If symptoms worsen, seek advice sooner rather than waiting for a product to work.
