Read the studies and evidence limits

Ask what was measured

A change in bacteria or a microscopic score is not automatically a change in smell, discomfort, or quality of life. A useful study needs to match the product, the people using it, and the result being promised.

StudyWhat it testedWhat it cannot establish
Oral trial, 200872 postmenopausal participants; GR-1/RC-14 capsules for 14 days. A laboratory score describing vaginal bacteria improved. This is called a Nugent score.Odor relief, long-term benefit, or gummy effectiveness. We accessed the abstract, not the full paper.
Vaginal trial, 201414 postmenopausal participants; short vaginal administration. Microbial changes occurred without significant Nugent-score or pH benefit.Benefit from taking oral capsules or gummies. This was a very small study.
Lactin-V trial, 2020A specific vaginal formulation after antibiotics in 228 premenopausal women with bacterial vaginosis (BV); fewer recurrences.A general menopause-odor claim for an over-the-counter oral supplement.

Compare five things before comparing brands

Compare the specific bacteria, the amount per serving, whether the product is swallowed or used vaginally, who was studied, and which symptoms were measured. A strain is a specific type of a microorganism. CFU, or colony-forming units, is a measure of the number of live microorganisms. A larger CFU number does not automatically mean a better result.

The CDC guideline for bacterial vaginosis (BV) does not support available probiotic products as a substitute for or addition to BV treatment, while discussing a specific promising vaginal formulation separately. That distinction matters.

Gummies need their own evidence

A gummy can use different organisms and added vitamins from a capsule sold by the same brand. A shared brand name does not make the products clinically interchangeable.
