Vaginal odor during menopause

Start by separating odor on the surrounding skin from a change in vaginal discharge. A body wash cannot tell you what is causing the change.

Sources checked November 12, 2026 · Not clinically reviewed

“Crotch smell after menopause”
r/Menopause · thread title · Direct quote
A scent is not a hygiene score.

A mild natural scent is normal. A new, strong, or persistent change is worth discussing, especially with other symptoms.

Start by separating the questions

People often use “down there” to describe several areas at once. The vulva is the external genital skin. The vagina is internal. Sweat on skin, urine-related concerns, and a change in vaginal discharge are different things to describe to a clinician.

You do not have to work out the cause yourself. Notice where you seem to detect the change, when it happens, and whether anything else feels different.

Where menopause fits

Lower estrogen can affect vaginal tissues and their environment. Dryness, discomfort with sex, irritation, and some urinary symptoms can be part of genitourinary syndrome of menopause, often shortened to GSM. Similar symptoms can have other causes, so menopause is context, not an automatic diagnosis.

What a wash can and cannot do

A tolerated external cleanser can remove surface residue during washing. It cannot establish why vaginal scent has changed, diagnose an infection, or replace treatment for a medical condition. More frequent washing or fragrance can add irritation without answering the underlying question.

Three useful next steps

Keep care simple

Water, gentle external cleansing, and avoiding scented products are a practical starting point.

Gentle vulva care

Describe the change

Bring timing, associated symptoms, and products you use to an appointment.

Open the checklist
Get symptoms checked

New or persistent odor, altered discharge, burning, itching, or pain should be assessed. Any bleeding after menopause needs evaluation. See symptoms that need attention.
