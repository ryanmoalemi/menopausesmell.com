URO gummies vs. capsules

The same brand sells two different formulas. Compare the ingredients and bacteria before choosing a format.

Sources checked November 12, 2026 · Not clinically reviewed

The short answer: these are different formulas, not the same supplement in two formats. Neither was established here as a treatment for menopause-related odor.

What is different?

At the time checked, URO capsules and URO gummies had different organisms, amounts, and added ingredients. They are not simply two ways to take the same formula.

These are label facts, not a dosing recommendation or proof that one formula is better. Formulas may change.

How to read the numbers

CFU means colony-forming units, a measure of live microorganisms. A strain is a specific type of a microorganism. A larger CFU number alone does not show that one product will work better.

What to compare on any supplement

  • Strain: look beyond “Lactobacillus” to an identifier such as GR-1.
  • Viable amount: check CFU, storage instructions, and whether the stated amount is maintained through expiration.
  • Added ingredients: gummies may add sugar, flavoring, or vitamins; capsules can have other added ingredients.
  • Evidence match: confirm the way it is taken, exact formula, people studied, and result measured in the cited study.
  • Purchase terms: compare one-time cost with the renewal price, serving count, and cancellation terms.
What neither format proves

Neither “gummy” nor “capsule” establishes relief from vaginal odor. Persistent symptoms need assessment, regardless of what a product advertises.
